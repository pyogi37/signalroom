# SignalRoom review workspace

## Mode

Operate

## Job

Help an AI solution engineer inspect how a discovery conversation became a proposed solution, resolve material gaps, and make a recorded human decision without leaving the workspace.

## Direction contract

**THESIS — The evidence docket.** The interface behaves like a live architecture-review packet: provenance, uncertainty, proposal, critique, and decision occupy stable regions that can be scanned in sequence.

**OWN-WORLD — Technical review practice.** It draws from architecture decision records, incident handovers, audit workpapers, and marked-up engineering specifications. Crisp ruled structure replaces cards; cobalt identifies the current decision; amber and red are reserved for risk.

**STORY — Evidence to judgment.** The reviewer enters at the human checkpoint, sees the graph's completed work, selects a requirement, reads its transcript evidence, checks the proposed architecture and critic pass, resolves unanswered questions, then approves or requests changes.

**FIRST VIEWPORT — A working brief, not a product pitch.** A compact task heading and workflow status lead directly into three stable columns: grounded requirements, the selected evidence docket, and the human decision panel. The next action is visible without scrolling.

**FORM — Ruled dossier.** Concept seed `3f691535`. Square-edged document surfaces, fixed information columns, restrained sans typography, tabular numerals, one-pixel rules, and state color only where it carries meaning. Motion is limited to the progress state and the new-discovery sheet.

## Proof sequence

1. Current human checkpoint and workflow completion.
2. Requirement with confidence and transcript citation.
3. Proposed architecture beside the critic pass.
4. Explicit unresolved questions and retrieved passages.
5. Grounding metrics, audit history, and the human approval control.

## Responsive behavior

Desktop preserves all three decision regions. Medium screens move the decision panel below the evidence while keeping it visually distinct. Small screens turn requirements into a horizontal selector, stack analysis sections, and keep controls at touch-friendly sizes.

## Known risk

High information density can feel administrative. Strong hierarchy, readable type, and a single accent keep the workspace calm rather than bureaucratic.
